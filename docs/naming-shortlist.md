# RememberMe Naming Shortlist

Living document. Updated as feedback comes in and screening advances.

**Status (as of September 27, 2026): no candidate is legally cleared.**
All trademark screening so far is preliminary web research — not official
USPTO/TSDR clearance and not legal advice. Final candidates go to the
UW Entrepreneurial Law Clinic / trademark counsel.

**Open brand-architecture question:** is the personality name separate from the
company brand (e.g. Kinsay the company, Fern the voice — the Alexa/Amazon
model), or does one name carry everything? Device naming matters: per Star,
the persona-style names double as the future device name; brand-style coinages
do not.

## Top 5

*Order revised Oct 3, 2026 per the naming-principles scorecard (see the Product Naming Principles brief). Votes unchanged — note the scorecard diverges from Star's Miri pick; that's a conversation.*

### 1. Fern
- **Roles:** Persona · Brand · Device
- **Why:** Olli's favorite, and the scorecard agrees. Arbitrary mark (a plant name for a storytelling app) — strong and ownable. Lives outside the crowded memory-word field where Remento/Meminto fight; the distinctiveness research says that white space is a strategic asset. Vivid imagery and a one-sentence story: ferns are among Earth's oldest plants — they endure. Cleanest preliminary screen of the persona names.
- **Watch-outs:** Soft "F" onset is a slightly weaker far-field wake word; fine for close-range hardware.
- **Votes:** Olli

### 2. Mabel
- **Roles:** Persona · Device
- **Why:** The phonetics research upgraded this one: m/b bilabials literally register as soft and warm, and the name derives from "lovable." Vintage-name-as-persona follows the Alexa/Siri/Muse pattern. Flatters an older storyteller instead of patronizing one; embosses nicely.
- **Watch-outs:** Nearby unrelated brand Mabel's Labels (non-competitive).
- **Votes:** Olli

### 3. Esme
- **Roles:** Persona · Device
- **Why:** Star's pick. French for "beloved" — the brand's emotional core in a single word. Soft, elegant phonetics match the warmth brief.
- **Watch-outs:** Sesame Workshop's *Esme & Roy* adjacency; counsel should assess.
- **Votes:** Star

### 4. Miri
- **Roles:** Persona · Device
- **Why:** Technically the best wake word on the list — two bright syllables, first-syllable stress. Short and modern.
- **Watch-outs:** Scorecard copycat flag: too close to **Siri** (Watkins' SCRATCH test). Weakest imagery of the persona names — no mental picture. The Hebrew "wonderful" claim still needs verification.
- **Votes:** Star (#1)

### 5. Kinsay
- **Roles:** Brand only
- **Why:** Warm, accessible coinage that says what it does.
- **Watch-outs:** The scorecard demotes it: "kin"+"say" is a transparent compound sitting near descriptive/functional territory, and it swims in the most crowded part of the field — weakest relative distinctiveness of the five. Phonetic proximity to **Keepsay** (copycat flag); reads as "Kinsey" (unfortunate homophone). Not a device name.
- **Votes:** Olli

## The rest, in ranking order

6. **June** — Persona · Device. Warmest and most familiar; every Legend already knows a June. One syllable is weak as a wake word; common word = weaker protection; adjacency with the game *June's Journey*.
7. **Everkin** — Brand only. Strong, enduring, premium-feeling master-brand alternative to Kinsay. Slightly less warm, a touch more corporate.
8. **Bequest** — Brand only. Star likes it for the company: a real word meaning legacy, distinctive in this space, serious embossed on a hardcover. Per Star, **not a device name.** Can read legalistic rather than warm.
9. **Odette** — Persona · Device. Elegant, literary (*Swan Lake*), great three-syllable wake word. Hardest to hear-once/spell-once for an older storyteller; feels more formal than familial.
10. **Yore** — Keepsake imprint / brand accent. Fits Dick's "reminiscence" theme; beautiful and poetic for the physical book. Too vague and common-word to carry the company alone.
11. **Folksay** — Brand only. Warm and plainspoken, but shares the "-say" suffix pattern with the eliminated Keepsay and feels the least premium of the coinages.
12. **Loquela** — Brand only. Latin for speech — distinctive and ownable. The hardest hear/spell on the list: a real liability for a voice-first product aimed at grandparents.
13. **Gleeman** — Brand only. Old English for a storyteller/minstrel, so the meaning is perfect. Obscure, leans masculine, and people will misspell it.

## New ideas from Dick's themes (not yet vetted)

Dick framed the app as: *Memory, Reflection, Reflections, Recall, Recollection,
Reminiscence, and Contemplation — life, or a moment in life.* His example
"MemoryMoment" points at descriptive compounds, but generic `mem-`
constructions are out per the naming requirements, so these stay in the spirit
rather than the letter:

- **Reverie** — a contemplative daydream; matches his reflection/contemplation
  framing. Adjacency: Reverie is a mattress/sleep brand — needs screening.
- **Halcyon** — evokes golden, idyllic memory; calm and premium. Needs screening.
- **Memora** — Latin, "things worthy of remembrance"; short and ownable.
  Needs screening.
